Quantum State Decoherence Mitigation via Cryogenic Topology

Filing Date 2023-11-14
Assignee Nexus Quantum Systems LLC
Classification G06N 10/00

The present invention relates generally to quantum computing architectures, and more specifically, to systems and methods for mitigating decoherence in superconducting qubits utilizing a novel cryogenic topological shielding mechanism.

FIG. 1: Schematic representation of the cryogenic topology.

Abstract Technical Field

As quantum processors scale beyond the 100-qubit threshold, environmentally induced decoherence remains the primary bottleneck for sustained fault-tolerant computation. Traditional shielding methods rely on macro-scale magnetic isolation, which introduces severe thermal gradients at millikelvin operational temperatures.

This disclosure details a micro-architectural approach wherein a niobium-titanium (NbTi) lattice is integrated directly at the interposer level, passively routing stray electromagnetic interference away from sensitive Josephson junctions while simultaneously acting as a dedicated thermal conduit.

memory Claim 1

A quantum processor assembly comprising: a qubit layer; and a shielding interposer layer, disposed adjacent to said qubit layer, comprising a topological arrangement of superconducting conduits configured to channel thermal phonons.

memory Claim 2

The assembly of claim 1, wherein the topological arrangement forms a Penrose tiling pattern to maximize surface area to volume ratio for phonon dissipation.
